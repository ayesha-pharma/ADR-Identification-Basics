# Causality Assessment

## Project Overview

This simulated pharmacovigilance project demonstrates the assessment of the causal relationship between a suspected medicinal product and a reported adverse reaction.

The cases were evaluated using clinical information, temporal relationship, dechallenge/rechallenge, alternative causes, and known drug-event associations.

> **Disclaimer:** These are simulated educational cases created for pharmacovigilance training and portfolio purposes. They do not contain real patient data.

---

# Case 1 – Amoxicillin and Skin Rash

## Case Information

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Case ID | SIM-CA-001 |
| Patient Age | 34 years |
| Sex | Male |
| Suspected Drug | Amoxicillin |
| Indication | Respiratory infection |
| Adverse Reaction | Generalized itchy rash |

## Clinical Timeline

- Amoxicillin was initiated for a respiratory infection.
- Generalized itchy rash developed approximately 4 days after treatment initiation.
- Amoxicillin was discontinued.
- The rash subsequently improved.
- Rechallenge was not reported.

## Causality Assessment

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Temporal relationship | Compatible |
| Alternative causes | No clear alternative cause reported |
| Dechallenge | Positive |
| Rechallenge | Not performed/reported |
| Known association | Clinically plausible |
| Final classification | **Probable** |

### Rationale

The reaction occurred after exposure to amoxicillin and improved after discontinuation. No clear alternative cause was reported, supporting a probable relationship between amoxicillin and the rash.

# Case 2 – Metformin and Gastrointestinal Symptoms

## Case Information

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Case ID | SIM-CA-002 |
| Patient Age | 45 years |
| Sex | Female |
| Suspected Drug | Metformin |
| Indication | Type 2 diabetes mellitus |
| Adverse Reaction | Nausea and diarrhea |

## Clinical Timeline

- Metformin was initiated at 500 mg twice daily.
- Nausea and diarrhea developed within several days.
- No specific alternative cause was identified.
- The dose was reduced.
- Symptoms subsequently improved.
- Rechallenge was not reported.

## Causality Assessment

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Temporal relationship | Compatible |
| Alternative causes | Possible but not established |
| Dechallenge | Positive following dose reduction |
| Rechallenge | Not reported |
| Known association | Clinically plausible |
| Final classification | **Probable** |

### Rationale

The symptoms appeared after initiation of metformin and improved following dose reduction. The temporal relationship and clinical course support a probable association, although alternative gastrointestinal causes should be considered.

# Assessment Criteria

## Certain

A reaction may be classified as **Certain** when:

- There is a clear temporal relationship.
- The reaction is consistent with the suspected drug.
- Other explanations are unlikely.
- The reaction improves after withdrawal.
- The reaction recurs following rechallenge, when applicable.

## Probable

A reaction may be classified as **Probable** when:

- The timing is compatible with drug exposure.
- The reaction is clinically plausible.
- The reaction improves after withdrawal or dose reduction.
- Alternative explanations are less likely.
- Rechallenge is not required.

## Possible

A reaction may be classified as **Possible** when:

- The timing is compatible with drug exposure.
- The drug could plausibly cause the reaction.
- However, alternative causes are also possible.
- Dechallenge/rechallenge information may be unavailable or inconclusive.
