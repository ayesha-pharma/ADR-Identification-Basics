# ADR Case Analysis

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates the identification and assessment of potential Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) using simulated patient case scenarios.

Each case was assessed based on:
- Patient and medication history
- Adverse event identification
- Temporal relationship
- Dechallenge/rechallenge information
- Possible alternative causes
- Identification of the suspected drug

> **Disclaimer:** These are simulated educational cases created for pharmacovigilance training and portfolio purposes. They do not contain real patient data.

---

# Case 1 – Methotrexate and Mouth Ulcers

### Patient Information

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Case ID | SIM-ADR-001 |
| Age | 52 years |
| Sex | Female |
| Indication | Rheumatoid arthritis |

### Medication History

| Medication | Dose | Route | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methotrexate | 10 mg once weekly | Oral | Rheumatoid arthritis |
| Folic acid | 5 mg once weekly | Oral | Supplementation |

### Adverse Event

**Mouth ulcers**

The patient developed mouth ulcers after initiating methotrexate therapy.

### ADR Assessment

| Parameter | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Suspected drug | Methotrexate |
| Adverse event | Mouth ulcers |
| Temporal relationship | Plausible |
| Dechallenge | Not reported |
| Rechallenge | Not reported |
| Alternative causes | Not reported |
| Overall assessment | Potential ADR |

### Rationale

Methotrexate was identified as the suspected drug because the adverse event occurred after exposure to the medication and is clinically compatible with methotrexate-associated oral mucosal toxicity.

The available information is insufficient to establish a definitive causal relationship.

# Case 2 – Amoxicillin and Skin Rash

### Patient Information

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Case ID | SIM-ADR-002 |
| Age | 34 years |
| Sex | Male |
| Indication | Bacterial respiratory infection |

### Medication History

| Medication | Dose | Route | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 500 mg three times daily | Oral | Respiratory infection |
| Paracetamol | 500 mg as required | Oral | Fever |

### Adverse Event

**Generalized itchy skin rash**

The patient developed a generalized itchy rash during treatment.

### Timeline

- Amoxicillin initiated on Day 1.
- Rash developed on Day 4.
- Amoxicillin was discontinued.
- The rash subsequently improved.

### ADR Assessment

| Parameter | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Suspected drug | Amoxicillin |
| Adverse event | Generalized itchy rash |
| Time to onset | Approximately 4 days |
| Temporal relationship | Plausible |
| Dechallenge | Positive – rash improved after discontinuation |
| Rechallenge | Not reported |
| Alternative causes | No alternative cause reported |
| Overall assessment | Potential ADR |

### Rationale

Amoxicillin was identified as the suspected drug based on the temporal relationship between treatment initiation and development of the rash.

Improvement following discontinuation provides additional supporting evidence. Other causes of rash should still be considered.

# Case 3 – Metformin and Gastrointestinal Symptoms

### Patient Information

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Case ID | SIM-ADR-003 |
| Age | 45 years |
| Sex | Female |
| Indication | Type 2 diabetes mellitus |

### Medication History

| Medication | Dose | Route | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metformin | 500 mg twice daily | Oral | Type 2 diabetes |
| Atorvastatin | 10 mg once daily | Oral | Dyslipidemia |

### Adverse Event

**Nausea and diarrhea**

The patient developed nausea and diarrhea after starting metformin.

### Timeline

- Metformin initiated on Day 1.
- Nausea and diarrhea developed within several days.
- Symptoms persisted while treatment continued.
- The metformin dose was subsequently reduced.
- Gastrointestinal symptoms improved following dose reduction.

### ADR Assessment

| Parameter | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Suspected drug | Metformin |
| Adverse events | Nausea and diarrhea |
| Time to onset | Within several days |
| Temporal relationship | Plausible |
| Dechallenge/Dose reduction | Symptoms improved following dose reduction |
| Rechallenge | Not reported |
| Alternative causes | Other gastrointestinal causes should be considered |
| Overall assessment | Potential ADR |

### Rationale

Metformin was identified as the suspected drug because the gastrointestinal symptoms developed after treatment initiation and improved following reduction of the metformin dose.

The temporal relationship and improvement following dose reduction support a possible association.

# ADR Identification Methodology

The cases were assessed using the following structured approach:

1. Identify the reported adverse event.
2. Review the patient's medication history.
3. Establish the timeline between drug exposure and event onset.
4. Identify the suspected medicinal product.
5. Assess dechallenge and rechallenge information when available.
6. Consider possible alternative causes.
7. Document the assessment based only on the information available.
